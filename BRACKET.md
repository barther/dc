# The bracket — how the family ranks the trip

The front page is a pitch: a highlight reel of everything Washington has for us,
no schedule. The schedule lives behind sign-in, and the family builds it by voting.

## The shape

1. **Sign in.** The family layer opens: dates, the bracket, the schedule, the log,
   the trophy case.
2. **Bart picks the leave-home date and the back-home date.** Both travel days are
   eaten by the train, so hotel nights = return − leave − 2. The calendar strip is
   the same fact drawn as a block.
3. **Everyone fills a bracket.** Same bracket for everyone, seeded from the regret
   list in the venue file. Sixteen slots plus a play-in for every contender past
   sixteen. One matchup per screen, this or that, saved as you go.
4. **Every completed ballot becomes a full ranking, 1 to N.** Averaged across
   ballots, the family's order is born. The planner packs it into days.
5. **Real life amends it.** Punt, mark done, not this day, weather swaps: all of
   live mode survives untouched.

## Contenders

A contender is a schedulable unit: a bundle (Capitol Hill, the memorial loop,
Christmas Washington, the Tidal Basin loop) or a standalone venue. Accessories
ride with their bundle and are never contenders. Structural days (the trains,
arrival night, the Capitol lit on arrival) never enter the bracket.

A bundle's seed is its best member's seed. Today that gives seventeen contenders,
seeded Capitol Hill first and the Spy Museum last. The engine is generic: N
contenders means a 16-bracket with N − 16 play-in games, so the roster can grow
without touching the bracket code.

## The draw

Standard sixteen: 1 v 16, 8 v 9, 5 v 12, 4 v 13, 6 v 11, 3 v 14, 7 v 10, 2 v 15.
Play-ins pair the lowest seeds for the lowest slots (17 plays 16 for the 16 slot).
After the semifinals, the two losers play a third-place game. Then the final.
Seventeen contenders is seventeen taps.

## From a bracket to a ranking, with no ties

- Champion 1, runner-up 2, third-place winner 3, third-place loser 4.
- Every other contender exited in some round. Within a round, each loser lost to
  a different winner, so rank a round's losers by how far their conqueror went.
  Losing in the quarterfinals to the eventual champion beats losing in the
  quarterfinals to a semifinalist.
- Later rounds rank above earlier rounds. Play-in losers are last.

This is a total order derived only from the picks already made.

## From ballots to the family's order

- Only completed ballots count. A half-finished bracket saves where it stopped and
  contributes nothing.
- Each contender's score is the mean of its rank across completed ballots. Lower
  is better.
- **Champion protection.** Every ballot's champion is locked to the top of the
  order. Averaging finds consensus; this is the representation clause, so nobody's
  one thing gets averaged into the middle and cut.
- Ties break by seed.
- Everyone's ballot weighs the same. Sam's included.
- Rerun replaces the ballot. The old one is gone; the log says who reran.

## What the planner does with it

The family order replaces the authored seeds and tiers. The planner owns pacing
and fit only: HI/LO pairing, bundles staying whole, the departure-morning valve,
what fits in N nights. Ranked contenders fill the days in order; whatever doesn't
fit is an idea. The "must-see things" count becomes the top thirteen of the
family's order. Champions can't be cut by a shorter trip.

A rerun during the trip reorders only what hasn't happened. Completed days are
locked.

## Trophies that fall out

- **Landslide** (trip): every ballot crowned the same champion.
- **Bracket Buster** (each): your champion wasn't the family's number one.
- **Cinderella** (trip): a 13-seed or lower made the family's top eight.
