Before the train

Paperwork that has to happen first

A Scout is supposed to have a counselor and a started blue card before the work counts. Most counselors accept work done in good faith beforehand, but requirement 7 says the plan is approved before the first frame, so that one needs a yes before Nov 28.

  1. A Photography blue card started in Scoutbook, with a counselor named. Railroading too if Sam wants the ride to count. Ask the Scoutmaster now, not the week of.
  2. Requirement 7b, the plan. Sam writes a shot list for a visual story called "Washington, for Christmas" and the counselor signs off before the trip. The shot list below is the draft; Sam edits it into his own words.
  3. The camera. A phone counts. Sam should know before he leaves how to lock exposure, turn the flash off, and shoot in low light, because half of this story happens after dark.
  4. Railroading Option B approval, only if he wants it. Requirement 7b, Railfanning, says "with your parent or guardian's and counselor's approval" before any of its four items.
  5. A pocket notebook. Requirement 7c asks him to explain his choices afterward. One line per frame, written that evening, is the whole trick.

Photography

The badge

Requirements 4, 5, and 7 are shooting assignments; 6 is an edit; 3 is the conversation about composition and light. The week hands Sam every one of them. One rule the official text adds: the story in requirement 7 must use images other than the ones shot for 4, 5, and 6, so the Capitol at night for 4b and the Capitol in the story are two different frames, not one photo doing double duty.

Railroading

Rides along

Sam didn't ask for this one, but the badge's two experience requirements are the trip and cost nothing. Requirement 2a wants a planned rail journey of 500 miles or more from a timetable; requirement 7b, the Railfanning option, wants a trip planned, ticketed, and ridden with two predicted intermediate arrivals. The Crescent is about 730 miles each way.

ReqIn WashingtonWhat Sam does
2aThe Crescent, both waysBefore leaving, Sam plans the trip from Amtrak's timetable: train numbers (Crescent 20 north, 19 south), departure and arrival times, and the class of service (sleeper). He also explains why Amtrak exists. That is the whole requirement.Direct hit
7b(4)The CrescentSame plan, one step further: predict arrival at two intermediate stations, Atlanta and Charlotte work, then check the prediction against the real thing from the roomette window and report what he saw. Bart buys the tickets; Sam does the planning.Direct hit
7b(1)American History Museum"Visit a railroad museum, historical display, or a prototype railroad-sponsored public event." The America on the Move hall has the 1401 steam locomotive, full size, indoors. Photograph it with permission and describe the photos, which is a Photography frame doing honest double duty across two badges.Counselor's call

The pamphlet's own timetable lesson is the Crescent. Southbound and westbound trains carry odd numbers and northbound even, which is why it's 20 up and 19 home; a stop with separate Ar and Dp times is five minutes or longer; and the conductor's seat check has no name on it, so it travels with him. An adult must buy the ticket. That's Bart.
