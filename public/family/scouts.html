Sam's Merit Badge Map

The same trip, read as a stack of blue cards. Seven badges have requirements a Scout can only do by going somewhere, and Washington happens to be where most of those somewheres are. Nothing here adds a stop. It tells Sam which stops to pay attention to, what to write down that night, and what the counselor will ask afterward.

Before the train

Paperwork that has to happen first

A Scout is supposed to have a counselor and a started blue card before the work counts. Most counselors accept work done in good faith beforehand, but four requirements Sam would be doing on the trip say "with your counselor's approval" right in the text, so those need a yes before Nov 28.

  1. Blue cards started in Scoutbook for Citizenship in the Nation, Railroading, Photography, and whichever of American Heritage, Aviation, and Art Sam actually wants. Ask the Scoutmaster now, not the week of.
  2. Citizenship in the Nation 6, the speech. "With your counselor's approval, choose a speech of national historical importance." Sam picks the Gettysburg Address or "I Have a Dream" and gets the approval before the trip, so he can read it where it was given.
  3. Art requirement 6 approval. The museum visit must be approved by the Art counselor in advance. Name the National Gallery.
  4. Railroading Option B approval. Requirement 7b, Railfanning, says "with your parent or guardian's and counselor's approval" before any of its four items. Sam picks Option B and gets the yes before the trip, or the ride doesn't count.
  5. Photography requirement 7 plan approval. Sam writes a shot list for a visual story called "Washington, for Christmas" and the counselor signs off before the trip. The site's photo hunt list is the shot list.
  6. Capitol tour through the representative's office (Alabama's 3rd district). Members' offices book constituent tours and are the only source of House and Senate gallery passes. Ask for both. Sam's letter for requirement 8 gets hand-delivered to the office the same morning.
  7. Supreme Court argument calendar. If the Court sits during the trip, the three-minute public line satisfies Law requirement 6a. Not on the board; only worth it if Sam bites.
  8. A pocket notebook. Almost every requirement ends "tell your counselor what you learned." Three sentences per site, written that evening, is the whole trick.

Citizenship in the Nation

Eagle-required

Requirement 7 says do two of four visits. Washington delivers all four, plus the raw material for 2, 3, 4, 6, and 8. This is the clean sweep you were told about, checked against the official text. What DC can't do is requirement 5, five days of national news, and the counselor conversations themselves. One tie worth making: the issue Sam picks from those five days of news is the "national issue" his requirement 8 letter has to be about, so he does 5 before the trip and delivers 8 on it.

Railroading

The badge's two experience requirements are the trip. Requirement 2a wants a planned rail journey of 500 miles or more from a timetable; requirement 7b, the Railfanning option, wants a trip planned, ticketed, and ridden with two predicted intermediate arrivals. The Crescent is about 730 miles each way.

ReqIn WashingtonWhat Sam does
2aThe Crescent, both waysBefore leaving, Sam plans the trip from Amtrak's timetable: train numbers (Crescent 20 north, 19 south), departure and arrival times, and the class of service (sleeper). He also explains why Amtrak exists. That is the whole requirement.Direct hit
7b(4)The CrescentSame plan, one step further: predict arrival at two intermediate stations, Atlanta and Charlotte work, then check the prediction against the real thing from the roomette window and report what he saw. Bart buys the tickets; Sam does the planning.Direct hit
7b(1)American History Museum"Visit a railroad museum, historical display, or a prototype railroad-sponsored public event." The America on the Move hall has the 1401 steam locomotive, full size, indoors, which is a historical display by any reading. Photograph or sketch it with permission and describe the photos. Union Station's holiday model trains don't qualify; that event isn't railroad-sponsored.Counselor's call
2b, 4eMetroPublic transit that runs on rail, and safety on a commuter system. Every Metro ride is a data point; the platform edge lights and the "stand back" rule are the answers.Supports

The pamphlet's own timetable lesson is the Crescent. The Railfanning chapter teaches timetable reading on Train 19: New York, Mile 0, departing 2:15 p.m., arriving New Orleans, Mile 1377, the next evening. Sam joins the same train at Anniston. Three things the pamphlet wants him to know: southbound and westbound trains carry odd numbers and northbound even, which is why it's 20 up and 19 home; a stop with separate Ar and Dp times is five minutes or longer; and the conductor's seat check has no name on it, so it travels with him. It also says an adult must buy the ticket. That's Bart.

Two Option B items complete requirement 7. 7b(4) is certain. If the counselor accepts the museum locomotive for 7b(1), the badge's field work is done on this trip and what remains is book work: freight types, signals, crossings, and Operation Lifesaver.

Photography

Three of eight requirements are shooting assignments, and the trip is a better assignment than anything at home. The visual story in requirement 7 needs the counselor's approval of the plan before the first frame.

American Heritage

The badge is six requirements now, and the site visits in requirement 4 all say "your area," so none of them point at Washington. What the trip does is put the badge's subjects in the room with Sam: the Declaration, the flag, the anthem, the people, and the Library of Congress's own song collection.

ReqIn WashingtonWhat Sam does
1National ArchivesRewrite "We hold these truths" through "new Guards for their future security" in his own words, as easy to understand as possible. Doing the reading in front of the parchment is the version he'll remember.Supports
3bAmerican History MuseumThe flag, the Pledge, the Great Seal, the motto, and the anthem: adoption, changes since, and influence today. The Star-Spangled Banner itself hangs here, the one the anthem is about. The Great Seal is all over the Capitol, and the motto is on every coin in his pocket.Supports
2aLincoln Memorial, American History MuseumOne of two options Sam needs from requirement 2, and the only one that needs no counselor approval. One political leader and one private citizen. Lincoln, and Mary Pickersgill, the Baltimore flagmaker who sewed the banner upstairs. Or the Wright brothers, two bicycle mechanics, at Air and Space.Supports
3aBefore the tripA United States topic currently in the news, tied to America's past. Same five days of news Sam is already reading for Citizenship in the Nation 5, and the same issue he writes his representative about. One week of newspapers, three requirements.Supports
5cLibrary of CongressFive popular songs from different periods and what each says about its time. The requirement's own resource is the Library of Congress "Songs of America" collection; Sam picks his five in the building that keeps them.Counselor's call

Citizenship in Society

Mostly conversation, but requirements 7 and 8 ask for a historical figure and an event that changed how society saw a group of people. The best rooms for both are on the Mall. The current scouting.org index lists this badge without the Eagle Required mark that the other three Citizenship badges carry, so it counts toward the elective total, not the required list.

ReqIn WashingtonWhat Sam does
8Lincoln Memorial, African American History MuseumThe 1963 March on Washington: study it with a parent, stand where the speech was given, then see the museum's account of it. Describe the event and what changed.Supports
7MLK MemorialIf no one local fits, the requirement allows a historical figure. The memorial's inscription wall is the interview: what inspired him, what he faced, why it worked.Supports

Sam punted the African American History Museum. That's his call and the planner honors it. If he wants requirement 8 to come from the trip, the MLK Memorial and the Lincoln steps cover it without the museum. If he's willing to bring it back as an idea, the museum makes the strongest version.
